Why the g4 RMII2 pinctrl group is WRONG for the AST2050 (source read of
drivers/pinctrl/aspeed/pinctrl-aspeed-g4.c):
  1. `#define RMII2_DESC SIG_DESC_BIT(HW_STRAP1, 7, 0)` — the g4 pinctrl gates
     RMII2 on SCU70[7]==0 (G4 semantics: bit7 alone selects RMII2). But on the
     G3, SCU70[8:6] is a 3-bit MAC-mode field; strap 110 (RMII1+RMII2) has
     bit7=1 (measured SCU70=0x00819582). So the g4 pinctrl reads "RMII2 NOT
     selected" and won't enable it — same class of G3-vs-G4 strap-layout bug
     as the GPIOF5 issue fixed by patch 0008, but here it mis-selects the
     whole function.
  2. The g4 RMII2 pins are D9/A10/B10/C9/A9/E8/D8 (GPIOT/GPIOV group) — the
     AST2400's RMII2 balls. The AST2050's RMII2 balls (schematic §7:
     A5/B5/B6/C4/D4/D5) are the **GPIOE group** (pin names MIIRXD2/RMII2RXD0/
     GPIOE2 …). Different hardware pins entirely.
  3. The GPIOE-as-MAC routing is gated by SCU74[27] ("Enable GPIOE group pins
     shared with MAC pins", datasheet §18) — but the datasheet notes it is
     "valid for SCU70[8:6]=2,4,7", NOT 6, so even setting it may not be the
     mechanism for strap=110.

Empirical test on the live board: set SCU74[27]=1 (SCU74 0x4204D000 ->
0x4A04D000) and re-triggered eth1 up -> STILL "No channel found". So SCU74[27]
alone is not the enable. Restored SCU74=0x4204D000.
